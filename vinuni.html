Workshop Design · Psychology Education · Career Development

VIN Your Mind: Explore the Psychology Journey

Anel Reed and I co-designed and facilitated an interactive workshop for undergraduate psychology students at VinUniversity in Vietnam. The workshop moved students from experiencing authentic research tasks to identifying their transferable skills and applying those skills to real-world career opportunities.

01 · Workshop Design

Designing a learning journey from research to career application

Rather than organizing the session as a traditional lecture, we designed a sequence of short presentations and hands-on activities that progressively asked students to apply what they were learning.

01

Experience

See how psychological research works and practice collecting behavioral data.

→
02

Interpret

Work with research findings and practice turning statistical relationships into meaningful explanations.

→
03

Identify

Recognize research, communication, critical-thinking, and collaboration skills developed through psychology.

→
04

Apply

Translate those skills into evidence of value for employers and future career pathways.

To give students a common language for thinking about their development, we connected VinUniversity psychology learning outcomes with APA 3.0 and international undergraduate psychology competencies. Students could then use those frameworks to recognize skills they had already developed through classes, projects, research, and extracurricular experiences.

Featured Workshop Tool

Mapping academic experiences into transferable skills

A class project, research assignment, presentation, or extracurricular activity can demonstrate skills that extend beyond the immediate assignment. The challenge is making that connection visible.

The Mapping My Skills activity gave students a structured way to connect what they had done with research, communication, critical-thinking, problem-solving, and collaboration competencies.

04 · Student Activities

Learning by doing

Each activity was designed to make a different part of the psychology learning process visible—from observing behavior and interpreting evidence to articulating transferable skills.

Activity 01

Mini Research Coding Challenge

Students watched a video from a developmental psychology study and coded a child's responses using a structured data-collection sheet.

Students practiced

Observation · Behavioral coding · Data collection · Following a research protocol

Debrief

Students compared their observations with the research team's coded data and discussed how raw behavior becomes analyzable information.

Activity 02

Data Detective Challenge

Students examined statistical relationships, identified meaningful patterns, and proposed possible explanations for why variables might be associated.

Students practiced

Data interpretation · Critical thinking · Statistical reasoning · Evidence-based explanation

Key concept

Students were explicitly asked to distinguish correlation from causation before sharing their interpretations.

Activity 03

Mapping My Skills

Students reflected on classes, research, projects, and extracurricular activities and connected those experiences with psychology learning competencies.

Students considered

Research skills · Communication · Critical thinking · Problem-solving · Cultural understanding · Collaboration

Activity 04

Job-Market Reality Check

Students contributed the skills they believed they had developed and then compared those responses with evidence about broader skill demand in the Vietnamese labor market.

Learning goal

Help students recognize how competencies developed through psychology could transfer to multiple professional contexts.

Activity 05

Pitch Your Mind: Skills-to-Market Challenge

Students formed small teams and reviewed real-world job postings. Each team selected a position that best aligned with its interests and experiences.

Teams then brainstormed how their psychology training could help them respond to an employer's needs.

“Why would you be an ideal candidate for this role?”

To make the task accessible to students who were still exploring career possibilities, we provided a structured translation process.

Experience

What did I do?

→
Skill

What ability did this require?

→
Evidence

How can I demonstrate it?

→
Employer Need

What does this role require?

→
Contribution

How could I use this skill there?

As a psychology student, I've learned to...

In my class / project / research, I...

This experience helped me develop...

In this role, I could use that skill to...

Teams presented their reasoning to the larger group, giving students practice communicating academic experience in language that connected directly with a workplace context.

05 · Workshop in Action

From individual reflection to collaborative practice

The workshop alternated between individual reflection, small-group problem solving, whole-group discussion, and student presentations.

Across the workshop, students moved between individual reflection, collaborative problem-solving, applied practice, and presentation.

06 · Design Principle

Reduce the distance between “what I studied” and “what I can do”

A central design challenge was helping students move beyond simply naming the courses and projects they had completed toward recognizing what those experiences demonstrated about their capabilities.

The workshop therefore did not simply tell students that psychology develops transferable skills. Activities repeatedly asked students to produce the connection themselves: observe behavior, interpret information, identify a skill, connect that skill to an experience, and explain how it could create value in another context.

Workshop Design Goal

Move students from “I took a psychology course” to “Here is what I learned to do, where I demonstrated it, and why it matters.”
