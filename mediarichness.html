CASE STUDY 01 · EXPERIMENTAL RESEARCH

How Communication Format Shapes Engagement and Trust

A controlled remote study examining whether receiving the same message through live video or voice-only communication changes engagement, belief revision, and behavioral reliance.

Role: Principal investigator and lead researcher

Timeline: 2022–2026

Participants: 62 children ages five and seven

Methods: Controlled experiment, behavioral observation, generalized mixed-effects models, and time-to-event analysis

The research problem

Communication platforms allow the same conversation to happen through video or voice only. Although the words may remain identical, removing video also removes facial expressions, eye gaze, gestures, and other cues that help people interpret a speaker’s attention and intentions.

I designed a controlled study to investigate one central question: When message content is held constant, does the communication channel change how children respond to and rely on a speaker?

What I wanted to understand

  • Would children be more likely to revise a belief after a video interaction?
  • Would communication format affect whether children questioned or protested against the speaker?
  • Would communication format influence how long children relied on the speaker’s promise?
